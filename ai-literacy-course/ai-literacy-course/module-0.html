0.1 — Why This Course Exists

By the end of this lesson: you can explain in your own words why a shared approach to AI matters more than everyone working it out alone.

1Hook

Imagine every lecturer had a completely different rule about AI. One bans it outright. One requires it. One has never mentioned it and marks as though it doesn't exist.

What would that be like to study in?

2Core idea

That scenario isn't hypothetical — it's what happens by default when a technology arrives faster than anyone can agree on what to do about it. The result is that students carry the cost: guessing what's allowed, getting it wrong through no fault of their own, and having no way to know in advance.

A shared approach isn't there to restrict you. It's there so the expectations are the same wherever you are on campus, and so you can know them in advance rather than after something goes wrong.

This course sets out what those shared expectations are, and — more usefully — builds the judgement to make good decisions in situations no rule anticipated.

0.2 — What's Expected of You

By the end of this lesson: you can state the core expectations around AI use, disclosure, and academic integrity.

1Hook

"If my lecturer doesn't ask whether I used AI, I don't need to say."

True or false?

2Core idea — myth and reality

Five assumptions worth correcting before you go further.

"If nobody asks, I don't need to say" ›

Transparency is the default, not something triggered by being asked. If AI shaped the work, say so.

"AI-assisted means not really mine" ›

Not true either. Work can be genuinely yours and AI-assisted — provided you did the thinking and you say what the tool did.

"If it's not flagged, it was fine" ›

Detection isn't the standard. Integrity is about what you actually did, not what was noticed.

"The tool is responsible for its mistakes" ›

You submitted it, so you're accountable for it. Module 1 covers this properly.

"Rules are the same in every course" ›

The baseline is shared, but individual courses can be stricter for good reasons. Check your course outline.

0.3 — Your Data, Your Rights

By the end of this lesson: you can describe what happens to information you enter into an AI tool, and decide what's safe to share.

1Hook

You paste a draft into a chatbot to get feedback. It includes a personal story about something difficult you went through.

Where does that story go?

2Core idea

Not all AI tools handle your information the same way. Broadly, there are two categories, and the difference matters.

Institutionally approved tools come with agreements about how data is stored, who can access it, and how long it's kept — obligations shaped by South Africa's data protection law (POPIA).

Free public tools may store what you type, use it to improve their systems, or make it visible to staff at that company. That's often disclosed somewhere in terms almost nobody reads.

A useful default: don't put anything into a public AI tool that you wouldn't be comfortable seeing outside your own control.

That includes: personal details about yourself or others, anything about someone's health, unpublished research, and material shared with you in confidence.

3Worked example — a quick decision
Ask first
Is this information sensitive — about a person, confidential, or not mine to share?
If yes
Use an approved tool, or remove the sensitive parts before pasting anything.
If no
You're probably fine — but check your course's own guidance, which may be stricter.
Still unsure
Ask your lecturer. Asking beforehand is always cheaper than fixing it afterwards.

0.4 — How This Course Works

By the end of this lesson: you can navigate the course and recognise a lecturer-linked assignment when you see one.

1The shape of it

Four modules after this one. Modules 1–3 are the core; Module 4 is optional and goes further for anyone who wants it.

Inside each module, three lessons build on each other in the same order every time:

Level 01 Understand Get the idea clearly before doing anything with it.
Level 02 Apply Use it on a real task in your own subject.
Level 03 Create Make something of your own with it.
2Assignments

Nothing in this course is graded by default, and nothing you type into a reflection box is stored or seen by anyone.

Separately, your lecturer can attach an assignment to any lesson. When they have, you'll see a marked banner at the end of that lesson with the brief and the due date. If there's no banner, there's no assignment.

Try the "Lecturer assignment preview" switch at the top of the page to see both states.
